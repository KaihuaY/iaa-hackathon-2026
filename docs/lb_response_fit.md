## 2. Derivation (5 lines)

1. Tweedie p=1.5 row deviance: d = -8 sqrt(y) + 4 y q^(-1/2) + 4 q^(1/2); LB = 1 - D/D0 is linear in y.
2. With q = q_ref e^delta and r = y/q_ref: d(delta) - d(0) = 4 sqrt(q_ref) [ r (e^(-delta/2) - 1) + (e^(delta/2) - 1) ].
3. Expanding: sqrt(q_ref) [ (1-r) delta/2 + (1+r) delta^2/8 ]; the linear weight (1-r) carries the unknown y, the curvature uses r ~ 1.
4. Over a random public fraction f: dLB = sum_k a_k beta_k - kappa * sum_i w_i (cosh(delta_i/2)-1), w = sqrt(q_ref), kappa = 8 f / D0 (one scalar); delta projected in the w-metric on line level and standardised tilt (log SI; vehicle_value_group for motor).
5. OLS in (a_k, kappa); per-direction optimum beta*_k = a_k / (kappa W_line / 4), W_line = sum_line w; SE of beta* by simulation from the OLS covariance.

## 4. Does the model-difference component have public value?

Model-difference = delta minus its level and tilt projections (eps). Its linear term is unidentifiable from the score alone (it needs the direction of y), so the test is whether eps costs the full quadratic penalty (kappa_eps ~ kappa) or less. See the excess table and the CV table above.

**Verdict.** (i) Penalty-only null: X10 differs from X21 by eps rms 0.062 and would be expected to lose 56e-5 if its difference were pure noise; it actually scored +5e-5, so the difference carries real signal (about as much as it costs): the null is the wrong one for two peer models. (ii) CV test: public differences between model files follow CV differences (slope 0.82 through the origin). Residuals LB - slope*CV for the only true peers (within 0.003 CV of X21): 1314_X6_blend3bag.c +10e-5, 1703_X10_blend4.csv +60e-5. Older, weaker models (B6, L6, X1) have residuals of -9e-4 to +2.4e-3 but are confounded with uncalibrated line levels, so they say nothing about family value. Conclusion: with two peer files the model-difference component shows no detectable public value beyond CV. A peer family should land within about +/-6e-4 of X21 on the public rows, the same order as the whole level/tilt gain (~7e-4) and not predictable from the level/tilt fit. The X10 +60e-5 is one observation (one eps direction, measured 7 times through its probes).
